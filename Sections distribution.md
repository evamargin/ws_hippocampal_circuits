**Section questions.** Three each. Bring answers in your own words; the figure is your slide.

**Arman**
1. "Orphans" of what? In one sentence: what makes CA2, the fasciola cinereum and the indusium griseum a family, and what makes them orphans?
2. Fig. 2A shows one gene, Amigo2, in four sections. What does the picture let you say? What can one gene not tell you? (Fig. 2B–C: look where the three clusters overlap.)
3. CA2 resists plasticity but is needed for social memory; the FC has place cells but is not needed for social memory. Why would anyone care about a region that "does not learn"? What changes for the circuit if CA2 and the FC are really one region?

**Stefanie**
1. The SuM is "rarely in hippocampal wiring diagrams". In one sentence: where does it send axons inside the hippocampus, and where not? Why does that pattern matter for the orphan story?
2. Fig. 2E–F: two SuM cell types, Tac1+ and Tac1−. What did each do when activated, to the mouse and to hippocampal spikes? What exactly was measured?
3. Running and theta usually go together. The SuM pulls them apart. Why is that interesting? What would you have assumed before reading?

**Nandini**
1. Box 1 names three states: theta, SIA, LIA. For each, one line: what the LFP looks like, what the animal is doing. Then redraw Fig. 1A from memory.
2. Box 2: the "online / offline" model has two states. Which of the three did it leave out, and what does the paper say got lost with it?
3. Fig. 1C: dentate spikes are as frequent as ripples. If so, why has almost everyone studied ripples and almost nobody dentate spikes? Guess first, then check what the authors say.

**Emma**
1. Half of CA2 pyramidal cells are suppressed during ripples and ramp up before them. What do these n/ramping cells encode, and in which states? Which result tells you they are a real population and not a recording artefact?
2. BARRs and SIA are defined by different measures, CA2 spiking vs CA1 LFP amplitude. The authors say they are "likely the same state". What would falsify that: which experiment, which outcome?
3. CA2 → CCK basket cell → CA1 silence. In wk 2 CA2 was a transition zone inside the loop. What does the circuit look like if CA2 is instead an output node to the lateral septum? Which anatomical claim from wk 2 does that rely on?

**Sotiris**
1. Two basket cells on the same soma. List the properties the paper uses to separate PV and CCK basket cells: spiking, membrane time constant, release timing, inputs. Which of these make the CCK cell "ideally suited" to a desynchronised LFP, and how, mechanistically?
2. Box 3: endocannabinoids shut CCK basket cell output while pyramidal cells are active. How does that turn CCK inhibition into a state switch rather than a dimmer? What would you expect during SIA in a CB1 knockout?
3. Npas4 raises CCK innervation, Fos raises PV innervation, over days. What does that do to "brain state" as a concept: can a single cell have a state membership? What experiment would show the membership changing?

**Pablo**
1. DS1 vs DS2: how are they defined operationally (sink layer, waveform, reversal across the granule cell layer)? Why does the definition matter for every closed-loop study that triggered on "any hilar peak"?
2. Closed-loop silencing during dentate spikes impairs recall; during ripples it does not. What is the control logic here, and which confound would you check first: timing, the stimulation itself, spread to other inputs?
3. Fig. 4B–C: during DS2 the decoded position matches the current location; during ripples it does not. What does that predict for a decoder trained on running and applied to rest? Write "realigning the map to current reality" as a model statement.
